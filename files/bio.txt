Dr. Yue Zhao is an Assistant Professor of Computer Science at the University of Southern California, where he leads the FORTIS Lab. He serves as Associate Co-Director of the USC Institute on Ethics and Trust in Computing for 2026-2027. His lab builds Auditable AI Systems across four audit questions: detecting anomalies in data, verifying outputs, auditing actions, and optimizing effort when no ground truth is available to check against. For each question, the work records what happened so that it can be reconstructed and checked after the fact. Auditability serves accountability and efficiency alike: the same evidence shows what a system did wrong and what it never needed to do. This is the lab's contribution to the broader field of AI Assurance. The program spans anomaly and outlier detection at scale, LLM evaluation where no reference answer exists, and the security and auditability of AI agents that act on their own. Its current frontier is agent auditability: open-source infrastructure that audits AI agents from before deployment to after a run. He also advises early-stage AI startups and is founding Auditable AI, a startup building the auditable layer for AI agents on an open-source stack developed by his lab.
Dr. Zhao has authored over 80 peer-reviewed papers. His open-source projects, including PyOD, ADBench, TrustLLM, agent-audit, and Aegis, together exceed 60 million downloads and 30,000 GitHub stars. As of August 2026, 5,560 public repositories and 159 packages depend on PyOD. PyOD is named by OpenAI, used by Apache Beam, Amazon, Walmart, Databricks, and the European Space Agency, and cited in 49 patents and five books. Official guidance names it across Europe, Asia, the Middle East, and Latin America, from defense ministries and central banks to national AI authorities. Brazilian federal and congressional procurement audits run it. A Bank of Thailand paper published by the Bank for International Settlements states an eventual goal of deploying ECOD in production against its regulatory credit data. TrustLLM is cited in a U.S. Senate committee report and a NIST special publication on adversarial machine learning. Both the 2025 and 2026 editions of the International AI Safety Report cite it. Japan's National Institute of Information and Communications Technology describes it in a cybersecurity deck, and its AI Security Evaluation Platform builds two of three selectable test sets on it. It is an official benchmark in all four editions of the Future of Life Institute AI Safety Index through Summer 2026. The U.S. Department of Defense CDAO Generative AI Responsible AI Toolkit recommends both. Therapeutics Data Commons, which he co-authored, supplied the training data for Google Research's Tx-LLM and the prediction tasks in Google DeepMind's TxGemma.
His awards include the NVIDIA Academic Grant Program Award, multiple Amazon Research Awards, the Capital One Research Award, and the Foresight Institute AI for Safety & Science Nodes Grant. NSF grants and a Meta sponsored research agreement fund the lab alongside them. Those NSF grants include both phases of Pathways to Enable Open-Source Ecosystems (POSE). AAAI selected him for New Faculty Highlights, and his papers have won a Best Short Paper Award at ACM SIGSPATIAL and a Second Prize CCC Award at the IEEE ICDM BlueSky Track. He serves as an Associate or Action Editor for Transactions on Machine Learning Research, ACM Transactions on AI for Science, IEEE Transactions on Neural Networks and Learning Systems, and the Journal of Data-Centric Machine Learning Research. At conferences he is an Area Chair for ICLR, ICML, and NeurIPS.

Dr. Yue Zhao is an Assistant Professor of Computer Science at the University of Southern California, where he leads the FORTIS Lab. He serves as Associate Co-Director of the USC Institute on Ethics and Trust in Computing for 2026-2027. His lab works on AI assurance: detecting anomalies in data, verifying outputs, and auditing actions when no ground truth is available to check against. His approach to it, published under the name Auditable AI Systems, builds each point so that what happened can be reconstructed and checked after the fact. It spans anomaly and outlier detection at scale, LLM evaluation where no reference answer exists, and the security and auditability of AI agents that act on their own. Its current frontier is agent auditability: open-source infrastructure that audits AI agents from before deployment to after a run. He also advises early-stage AI startups and is founding Auditable AI, a startup building the auditable layer for AI agents on an open-source stack developed by his lab.
Dr. Zhao has authored over 80 peer-reviewed papers. His open-source projects, including PyOD, ADBench, TrustLLM, agent-audit, and Aegis, together exceed 60 million downloads and 29,000 GitHub stars. PyOD is named by OpenAI, used by Apache Beam, Amazon, Walmart, Databricks, and the European Space Agency, and cited in 49 patents and five books. Official guidance names it across Europe, Asia, the Middle East, and Latin America, from defense ministries and central banks to national AI authorities. Brazilian federal and congressional procurement audits run it. TrustLLM is cited in a U.S. Senate committee report, a NIST special publication on adversarial machine learning, and the International AI Safety Report 2026. It is an official benchmark in all four editions of the Future of Life Institute AI Safety Index through Summer 2026. The U.S. Department of Defense CDAO Generative AI Responsible AI Toolkit recommends both. Therapeutics Data Commons, which he co-authored, supplied the training data for Google Research's Tx-LLM and the prediction tasks in Google DeepMind's TxGemma.
His awards include the NVIDIA Academic Grant Program Award, multiple Amazon Research Awards, the Capital One Research Award, and the Foresight Institute AI for Safety & Science Nodes Grant. NSF grants and a Meta sponsored research agreement fund the lab alongside them. AAAI selected him for New Faculty Highlights, and his papers have won a Best Short Paper Award at ACM SIGSPATIAL and a Second Prize CCC Award at the IEEE ICDM BlueSky Track. He serves as an Associate or Action Editor for Transactions on Machine Learning Research, ACM Transactions on AI for Science, IEEE Transactions on Neural Networks and Learning Systems, and the Journal of Data-Centric Machine Learning Research. At conferences he is an Area Chair for ICLR, ICML, and NeurIPS.

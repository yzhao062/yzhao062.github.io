Shared by the `post-to-x` and `post-to-linkedin` skills. This is one example of a
short release post that explains use and value. It is not an endorsement of the
project, not a template to copy line by line, and not evidence that any feature of
the post caused its engagement.

## What the Post Said (Paraphrase)

The author announced that PrefixSim is now open source: a fast simulator for KV-block
prefix caches in LLM serving, released together with two datasets. With it, a reader
can replay real production traces and compare more than 40 eviction policies, judged
by compute savings (prefill work avoided) rather than by hit ratio alone. The post gave
one GitHub link (cacheMon/PrefixSim) and closed with four tags: LLM, PrefixCaching,
Systems, and OpenSource.

## What Is Worth Learning

- **Use and value come first.** A reader learns what the tool is, what they can do
  with it, and why the chosen metric matters, in a few sentences.
- **One concrete distinction.** Compute savings versus hit ratio gives the post a
  technical point without a feature list.
- **One link.** The repository carries the detail.
- **A few relevant tags.** Each tag names something the post is about.
- **No padding.** No bullet list, no rhetorical question, no research-program framing,
  no caveat paragraph, and no call to action were needed.

## What Not to Copy

- Do not reuse its wording, its sentence order, or its exact tag count.
- Do not treat its engagement numbers as a target or as proof that short posts or four
  hashtags perform better.
- A post with a different story (a paper finding, a talk, a reflection) may need a
  different shape. Use this example to calibrate brevity, not to force a format.

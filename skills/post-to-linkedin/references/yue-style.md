# Yue Zhao's LinkedIn voice

Use these patterns for Yue's personal research posts. They come from his published
feed and from his manual revision of the Auditable Agents summit post on 2026-09-10.
They describe editorial choices, not a rigid template.

## Voice patterns (observed; refine as posts accumulate)

1. **Open with a real event or consequence.** A presentation, invitation, community
   response, release, or deadline gives the technical point a reason to matter now.
2. **Move quickly to a concrete research problem.** Use a realistic action, number, or
   failure before introducing the framework.
3. **Enumerate the questions.** Yue often clarifies a research space by numbering the
   questions and then adding the practical dimension that the paper connects to, such
   as efficiency or cost.
4. **Name the conceptual distinction directly.** Short emoji-led lines work well for
   paired or tripled concepts. Keep the keywords themselves in ordinary text.
5. **Connect the artifact to the research program.** When useful, pair the paper or
   project link with the durable Auditable AI Systems or ecosystem page. The post
   should explain the relationship rather than merely listing links.
6. **State what remains open.** Yue's voice can be promotional while still saying that
   the complete end-to-end result has not been demonstrated.
7. **Invite a substantive response.** End with one technical question, request for a
   missing reference, or concrete action.
8. **Use hashtags narrowly.** Default to three topic tags. Add a fourth only when the
   post actually develops that topic; `#Optimization` belongs only if optimization
   appears in the body.

## Layout

- Use one or two sentences per block.
- Group numbered questions and concept definitions into scan-friendly clusters.
- Use emoji to label meaning, not to decorate every paragraph.
- Let a poster, figure, or linked page carry detail that would make the copy dense.
- In the browser composer, visually inspect whitespace. Use U+200B-only spacer lines
  between major blocks if LinkedIn collapses ordinary blank lines.

## Links and mentions

- Prefer one primary paper or project URL. Add one program-level hub when it explains
  the larger research direction.
- Mentions are optional. A real mention must be selected from LinkedIn's typeahead.
  Do not leave a literal `@Name` or choose an uncertain identity.
- Do not force an author list into every post. Credit can live in the paper or attached
  poster when the post is about the research idea rather than an acceptance notice.

## What not to learn from a single manual edit

- Correct spelling, grammar, stray spaces, and singular/plural agreement in future
  drafts. Preserve them only when recording an already published post verbatim.
- Do not add an efficiency or cost question unless the source or Yue's framing
  supports it.
- Do not make every post start with an invitation or use the same emoji sequence.

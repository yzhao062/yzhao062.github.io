# Yue Zhao's LinkedIn Voice

Use these patterns for Yue's personal research posts. They come from his published
feed and from his manual revision of the Auditable Agents summit post on 2026-09-10.
They describe editorial choices, not a rigid template.

## Default: Brief and Natural

Most posts should be short: a few sentences that say what the work or event is, what a
reader can do with it or learn from it, and why it is useful, followed by one link and
a few relevant hashtags. Do not pad a post to make it look substantial. LinkedIn's
3,000-character limit is capacity, not a target.

Write a longer post only when Yue asks for one or the content needs it, for example a
reflection that makes an argument or an event that changed how he frames a problem.
The summit post below is an example of that longer case.

See `skills/post-to-x/references/example-prefixsim.md` for one short release post used
as an editorial reference. Treat it as a calibration point for brevity, not a template
or evidence that its length or tags caused its engagement.

## Voice Patterns (Observed; Refine as Posts Accumulate)

1. **Open with the concrete thing.** A release, finding, presentation, invitation, or
   community response gives the post a reason to exist now.
2. **Explain use and value plainly.** Say what a reader can do with the artifact or
   what the finding changes. A realistic action, number, or failure helps more than a
   framework name.
3. **Name one distinction when it helps.** A single contrast (for example, the metric
   that matters versus the usual one) often carries the technical point.

The following moves fit some longer posts. Use each only when the content calls for
it; none is required:

- numbering the open questions in a research space;
- short emoji-led lines for paired or tripled concepts, with the keywords in ordinary
  text;
- linking the artifact to the Auditable AI Systems or ecosystem page, when the post is
  about that program and the link explains a relationship;
- ending with one technical question, request for a missing reference, or concrete
  action.

Do not add bullets, feature lists, questions, research-program framing, caveats, or a
call to action by habit.

Honest limits are a separate, required rule at any length: state what remains open
when the post would otherwise overstate the result (for example, separately validated
components presented as a complete system), and keep that limit inside the claim.

## Repost Captions

When resharing someone else's post, add one useful angle the original does not state:
who should use the work, the setting it fits, or a concrete connection to a problem.
Do not restate the original title, summary, or feature list. One to three sentences is
usually enough, and hashtags are optional. Use LinkedIn's native repost flow in the
browser composer so the original post is attached; a caption posted as a new post
must include the original URL.

## Layout

- Use one or two sentences per block.
- Use emoji to label meaning, not to decorate every paragraph. Many short posts need
  none.
- Let a poster, figure, or linked page carry detail that would make the copy dense.
- In the browser composer, visually inspect whitespace. Use U+200B-only spacer lines
  between major blocks if LinkedIn collapses ordinary blank lines.

## Links and Mentions

- Prefer one primary paper or project URL. Add a program-level hub only when the post
  is about that direction.
- Mentions are optional. A real mention must be selected from LinkedIn's typeahead.
  Do not leave a literal `@Name` or choose an uncertain identity.
- Do not force an author list into every post. Credit can live in the paper or attached
  poster when the post is about the research idea rather than an acceptance notice.

## Hashtags

Use a few tags that name what the post is about, usually two to four and never more
than five. Each tag must match the body; `#Optimization` belongs only if optimization
appears in the post. Choose for relevance. Do not claim that a tag count helps or hurts
distribution.

## What Not to Learn from a Single Manual Edit

- Correct spelling, grammar, stray spaces, and singular/plural agreement in future
  drafts. Preserve them only when recording an already published post verbatim.
- Do not add an efficiency or cost question unless the source or Yue's framing
  supports it.
- Do not make every post start with an invitation or use the same emoji sequence.
- Do not treat the summit post's length or seven-part structure as the default.

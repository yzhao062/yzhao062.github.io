Method note: a phrase counts as prior use when an organization uses it in a title, named
program, framework, standard, company, product, or recurring headline. Incidental prose
counts separately. This records public use on a date. It does not establish ownership,

## The three that are closed

**AI Auditing** is a profession, not an available label. The Institute of Internal Auditors
dates its Artificial Intelligence Auditing Framework to 2017 and still ships it. ISACA
published *Auditing Artificial Intelligence* by January 2019. The UK Information
Commissioner's Office issued *Guidance on the AI auditing framework* in February 2020, and
the European Data Protection Board runs a project titled exactly *AI Auditing*. Stanford,
ECAT, and the IAAA jointly ran a Conference on AI Auditing in June 2025.

**AI Assurance** is a named government programme. The UK Centre for Data Ethics and
Innovation published *The roadmap to an effective AI assurance ecosystem* in December 2021.
It then launched an AI Assurance Programme under that name. DSIT continued the label through
a September 2025 roadmap. NIST proposed formalizing the term in March 2022. MITRE published an
AI Assurance process in June 2024. PwC launched an assurance service line in June 2025.

**Auditable AI** collides with company names. FICO used the capitalized label in June 2021.
AuditableAI Inc. holds the compressed form as a company and product name. Rezolve AI
announced a technology called Auditable AI in July 2026. A separate consultancy operates at
`auditable.ca`. ISACA uses the phrase definitionally.

## Auditable AI Systems

The claim is stronger than a loose phrase match and stronger than the earlier run recorded.

BSI, Fraunhofer HHI, and the TUV Association jointly published two whitepapers sharing the
title stem. *Towards Auditable AI Systems: Current status and future directions* came in May
2021, and *Towards Auditable AI Systems: From Principles to Practice* in May 2022. The
second cites the first. TUV AI.Lab reused the stem for a 2024 conference report on LLM
auditing. Fraunhofer HHI still runs an Auditing and Certification of AI Systems programme
that names both papers. Separately, Graupner, Pawlaszczyk, and Hummert published *Basics of
Auditable AI Systems* in the 2023 IEEE CSCE proceedings.

Two corrections to what an earlier round of this run had recorded. TUV Association is a
co-publisher, not merely BSI and Fraunhofer. And no formal collection is named "the Towards
Auditable AI Systems whitepaper series"; the accurate description is a recurring title stem
across two joint whitepapers and a later report.

These sources establish prior institutional use in certification and conformity assessment.
That community carries different search intent from machine-learning research, so the phrase
is unlikely to mislead. They do not establish current ranking difficulty. Measure that with
dated, controlled retrieval queries and keep the returned URLs and positions.

## Agent Auditability

Thinnest of the five, and no longer unclaimed.

`aas-1.org` publishes an **Agent Auditability Standard**, version 0.1 dated May 2026, with
public comment through July 2026. The publisher is a private Bermuda company, so this is a
self-published draft rather than an accredited consensus standard, but it holds the exact
name.

IBM's December 2025 article on trustworthy agents names "the five layers of agent
auditability". The phrase is not its title, but the use is earlier than most and the source
is prominent.

**Priority note worth carrying into paper work, not naming work.** IBM's December 2025 "five
layers of agent auditability" predates the April 2026 *Auditable Agents* paper's "five
dimensions of agent auditability" by four months. The two are independent framings that
happen to land on the same count and a near-identical phrase. A reviewer may notice. Citing
or distinguishing it is cheaper than being asked about it.

## How to read this for a decision

Every one of these phrases is claimed, so a rule of "choose the unclaimed one" returns
nothing. The rows above establish prior use and no more. Separate the three questions and
answer each with its own evidence.

**Field confusion.** Compare the meaning each claimant attaches to the phrase against the
meaning intended. A term whose sense has been fixed by a certification body or a regulator
will be read in that sense.

**Discoverability.** This is a retrieval question and the table cannot answer it. Run dated,
controlled queries for the candidate and for the control, and keep the returned URLs and
positions. Personal equity in a phrase is measurable; institutional prior use is not a proxy
for it.

**Durability.** Name the event that would make each candidate stale, and give the evidence
that the event is likely. Prior use has little bearing here.

Say which question is being priced before quoting any row as an argument.

Research Summary

Auditable AI Systems. When something goes wrong, someone has to be able to say what happened and who is answerable. AI systems that act on their own can make that hard: the damage shows up, the path to it often does not. My lab builds three things: tools that catch unsafe permissions before an agent ships, a checkpoint that inspects tool calls before they run, and records good enough to piece together afterward who did what.

One question, asked at three levels: did this depart from what was expected, and can anyone still tell? At the action level, whether a call can be stopped before it runs, whether the system stayed inside its job and its permissions, and what evidence is left afterward. At the behavior level, whether the model still does what it is supposed to, both in ordinary conditions and under attack. At the distribution level, whether the input is the kind of thing the system was built and tested for. A departure can start at any level and can travel: an unfamiliar input can surface as a model failure, and a model failure can surface as an action nobody authorized. Neither step is guaranteed, and an action can go wrong with no model failure behind it. The order above runs the other way, from the consequence a person feels back toward where it may have started. Evidence has to survive the levels an incident touches, or the trail stops short of the origin.

Auditing Across the Agent Lifecycle

Auditable Agents is our framework for what agent auditability requires: five dimensions and three mechanism classes (detect, enforce, recover). The research below instantiates them across an agent's life. arXiv.

Pre · before deploy
FORTIS: a benchmark for over-privilege in agent skills.
agent-audit: a static scan of agent code for security and over-privilege.
Live · while running
Cross-User Contamination: one user's data leaking into another's in shared-state agents.
Topology Matters: measuring memory leakage in multi-agent LLMs.
Aegis: a pre-execution firewall that gates each tool call.
Post · after a run
Implicit Execution Tracing: recovering which agent caused a harmful result after the logs are gone.
GRADE: localizing the faulting step in a completed multi-agent run.
  1. Agent Action: Auditability and Control of Agent Systems

    At the agent action level, deployed systems act through tools, APIs, and shared state, so what a system does becomes the primary failure surface. I work on auditability frameworks (reconstruction, checking, and attribution of agent behavior after deployment), runtime control surfaces that intercept tool calls before they fire, agent-specific failure modes (over-privilege, cross-user contamination, the autonomy tax of defense training), and category-adaptive training-time defenses. These span the full lifecycle: pre-deployment audit, runtime tool-call mediation, and post-run attribution and recovery. My Auditable Agents position paper organizes these into five dimensions of auditability and three mechanism classes (detect, enforce, recover). These methods are moving from research into deployable open-source infrastructure that audits agents from before deployment to after a run.

    Keywords: Agent Auditability, Runtime Audit, Pre-Execution Mediation, Agent Failure Modes, Over-Privilege, Cross-User Contamination, Defense Training, Behavior Verification, Multi-Agent Systems, AI Agent Security

  2. Model Behavior: Trust and Robustness of Foundation Models

    Between input distribution and agent action, foundation models add their own failure surface: behavior under attack, hallucination of fact and reasoning, and adversarial robustness of retrieval. Failures on any of these surfaces can propagate downstream when the model's output is passed to an agent or a user-facing system. My lab's targeted work measures these surfaces through jailbreak detection for vision-language models, causal analysis of hallucination, query-agnostic attacks on retrieval-augmented generation, and LLM-as-anomaly-detector benchmarks. Community efforts like TrustLLM are tracked as collaborative consortium contributions, separate from lab-led work.

    Keywords: Foundation-Model Trust, Trustworthiness Benchmarks, LLM Hallucination, Jailbreak Detection, RAG Security, Adversarial Retrieval, Vision-Language Models, LLM-as-Judge

  3. Input Distribution: Anomaly and Out-of-Distribution Detection

    At the input distribution level, the question is whether an input departed from the conditions represented during development and evaluation. That boundary is hard to estimate when labels or representative examples are sparse. This is the lab's methodological and open-source base: the PyOD ecosystem and ADBench, benchmark infrastructure for NLP, automatic OOD detector selection, modality-specific OOD methods for graphs, multimodal data, and video, and few-shot cross-domain OOD detection. PyOD is the flagship open-source artifact and is now in its third major release. When an incident involves distribution shift, these methods supply the evidence for whether unfamiliar input conditions contributed to it; an action-only incident is established from evidence at the agent action level evidence instead. Current research emphasis sits at model behavior and agent action.

    Keywords: Anomaly Detection, Outlier Detection, Out-of-Distribution Detection, PyOD Ecosystem, Benchmark Design, Multimodal OOD, Graph OOD, Few-Shot OOD, OOD Detector Selection

Biography. Dr. Yue Zhao is an Assistant Professor of Computer Science at the University of Southern California, where he leads the FORTIS Lab. He serves as Associate Co-Director of the USC Institute on Ethics and Trust in Computing for 2026-2027. His lab builds methods, benchmarks, and open-source infrastructure that make AI systems auditable. This work detects and controls departures from expected behavior at three levels: agent action, model behavior, and input distribution. The program extends a decade of research on anomaly and outlier detection at scale. Its current frontier is agent auditability: open-source infrastructure that audits AI agents from before deployment to after a run. He also advises early-stage AI startups and is founding Auditable AI, a startup building the auditable layer for AI agents on an open-source stack developed by his lab.

Dr. Zhao has authored over 80 peer-reviewed papers. His open-source projects, including PyOD, ADBench, TrustLLM, agent-audit, and Aegis, together exceed 60 million downloads and 29,000 GitHub stars. PyOD is the subject of five published books, is named by OpenAI, and is used by Apache Beam, Amazon, Walmart, Databricks, and the European Space Agency. It is also named in the official guidance of defense ministries, central banks, financial regulators, and national AI authorities across Europe, Asia, and the Middle East. TrustLLM is cited in a U.S. Senate committee report, a NIST special publication on adversarial machine learning, and the International AI Safety Report 2026. It serves as an official benchmark in all four editions of the Future of Life Institute AI Safety Index. Both PyOD and TrustLLM are recommended in the U.S. Department of Defense CDAO Generative AI Responsible AI Toolkit.

His awards include the NVIDIA Academic Grant Program Award, multiple Amazon Research Awards, the Capital One Research Award, and the Foresight Institute AI for Safety & Science Nodes Grant. NSF grants and a Meta sponsored research agreement fund the lab alongside them. AAAI selected him for New Faculty Highlights, and his papers have won a Best Short Paper Award at ACM SIGSPATIAL and a Second Prize CCC Award at the IEEE ICDM BlueSky Track. He serves as an Associate or Action Editor for Transactions on Machine Learning Research, ACM Transactions on AI for Science, IEEE Transactions on Neural Networks and Learning Systems, and the Journal of Data-Centric Machine Learning Research. At conferences he is an Area Chair for ICLR, ICML, and NeurIPS.
